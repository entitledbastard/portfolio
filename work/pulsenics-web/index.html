The challenge

A technical product, for scientists and engineers

Pulsenics sells electrochemical impedance spectroscopy (EIS) hardware to battery, hydrogen and fuel cell teams. Its buyers are scientists and engineers. The site has to explain the products and keep up with a steady stream of news and studies.

I inherited the site from its original designer and kept its established look. Since then most of the pages have been redone or added as the company launched new products and needed new ways to explain its technology.

Information architecture

With Marc Varone

Help each visitor find the product they need

Pulsenics wanted to add a lot of new pages, and a new homepage hero. Rather than tack them on, Marc and I pushed for a workshop with their marketing team around one question: when a potential client lands on the site, what should they see, and how do we help them? The answer was that people didn't understand what each product was for. So we planned the site around two kinds of visitor.

From there we built two trees: one for the products and one for the industries they serve. Every industry splits into R&D and QC work, with room for case studies as Pulsenics publishes them.

Then we mapped every application to the person who would use the product, the person who would buy it, and the product that fits.

That map became the Applications page, which was our idea. It's where visitors decide which product is right for them. Each application points to one product, so nobody has to work out the product range on their own.

Homepage and product pages

With Marc Varone
01

Lead with the newest product

The homepage opens on AccelaGrade, the company's new battery grading system, with two clear actions: explore the product or download the spec sheet. It's their latest product, so it gets the top spot.

02

Two product lines, compared at a glance

AccelaGrade and the Pulse Series sit side by side in matching cards: a short line on what it's for, four capabilities each, and a single Learn More link. It's easy to see which one you need.

03

Product pages organised around real applications

The Pulse Series page explains the product through the problems it solves: degradation benchmarking, post-field failure analysis, catching stack defects before they ship. Each one links to a detailed Knowledge Centre article, so each product page leads into the detail behind it.

04

Give the software its own section

Every Pulse Series system ships with the Pulse Hub software, so the product page gives it a section of its own, shown on a monitor the way a lab would use it. The interface on that screen is the System Software I designed with Marc Varone.

The Knowledge Centre

I built the Knowledge Centre to improve the way Pulsenics publishes what it knows about EIS. It reads like documentation: how the technology works, then how it's used in battery and hydrogen work. It's all built on the Webflow CMS, so anyone on the team can write and publish articles without touching the layout.

  • Left

    Navigate by topic

    Technology basics in order, then applications grouped by industry: battery and hydrogen.

  • Centre

    Read in depth

    Long articles with equations, figures and interactive charts inline. The Pulsenics team built the charts; I host them and embed them in the articles.

  • Right

    Jump within a page

    An automatic table of contents for each article, plus a link to talk to Pulsenics.

News

Pulsenics publishes partnerships, conference talks, product launches and studies almost every month. The News page has a featured story on top and a filterable grid underneath: Announcement, Conference, Technology, Company, Collaboration, and Studies & Papers.

It runs on the CMS like the Knowledge Centre. Someone on the team adds a story, and it shows up on the News page and in the latest news banner at the top of the homepage. Nobody needs me to keep the site current.
